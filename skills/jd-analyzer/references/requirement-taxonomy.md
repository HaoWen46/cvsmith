# Requirement taxonomy

Classify what affects application viability and resume selection; ignore language that merely describes the employer.

## Gate

A gate passes all four tests: the answer is binary, an external fact settles it, tailoring cannot change it, and a `no` disqualifies even the strongest applicant.

Typical gates are work authorization or sponsorship limits, security clearance, legally required licensure, date-bound enrollment or graduation eligibility, and hard location or relocation constraints.

Not gates: years of experience, seniority labels, tools, domain familiarity, degree-field preferences, and any “or equivalent” condition; these vary by evidence and employer discretion.

## Must-have

A must-have describes the work or capability central to the role; rank it higher when it appears in the title, repeats across sections, carries explicit required language, is unusually specific, or appears early in responsibilities or qualifications.

Responsibilities often reveal the real priority better than a flat technology list; combine repeated statements into one requirement while citing each source line.

Years asked are evidence of intended level, not a binary bar; strong direct scope may compensate, so keep them ranked and note the stretch.

## Nice-to-have

Preferred, bonus, “a plus,” late single-mention tools, and team-stack descriptions are usually nice-to-have unless repetition or role scope makes them central.

Long wish lists should not receive equal weight; identify the few capabilities the actual work depends on.

## Non-requirement

Benefits, EEO and legal boilerplate, generic values, “fast-paced,” “passionate,” and mission language do not consume resume space.

Operational constraints such as travel, on-call, or schedule belong in notes unless they satisfy the strict gate test.

## Level decoding

- Intern or new-grad: assist, learn, contribute within a project, mentorship expected.
- Junior or mid: own and ship a feature, service, analysis, or operational result end to end.
- Senior: resolve ambiguity, design systems, lead cross-team execution, and multiply others.
- Staff or principal: set technical direction and create organization-level leverage.
- Manager: deliver through hiring, development, prioritization, and team outcomes.

When title and scope conflict, report the scope-implied level and the contradiction.

## Evidence targets

- Tool or stack: a visible bullet where it performs load-bearing work.
- Practice: an artifact or mechanism such as a test suite, evaluation, deployment, review process, or runbook plus its effect.
- Scale: a defensible quantity that changes the reader's understanding.
- Domain: domain-specific work in context, not an acronym in skills.
- Collaboration: the other function and what changed through that interaction.
- Communication: the actual document, presentation, publication, or audience result.

The acceptance test is whether a skeptical interviewer would consider the requirement addressed and know what to probe next.
