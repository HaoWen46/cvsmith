# Field guide: research and early academic applications

Use this for graduate programs, research internships, fellowships, research-assistant roles, and postdoctoral applications; it is not a full senior-academic CV system.

Faculty and research readers look for research direction, concrete process, outputs, difficult coursework, and evidence that the candidate can handle open-ended work.

## Evidence order

1. Publications, posters, submissions labeled with their actual status, and the candidate's contribution.
2. Experiments, datasets, baselines, methods, infrastructure, and results produced in a lab or independent project.
3. Advisor, lab, and collaborator context that helps the reader place the work.
4. Teaching scope, course, students, materials, and measured teaching outcomes.
5. Hard relevant coursework, marks, honors, and industry work that demonstrates research-enabling engineering.

Group every experience entry as `research`, `teaching`, or `industry` when those distinctions matter; the renderer emits separate standard sections in that order.

Use `grad-applicant`, `phd-applicant`, or `postdoc` for the actual target level; undergraduate research programs can use `intern` with `target_field: academic`.

Put a thesis or degree advisor on the degree line only when it naturally belongs there, and put a research lab or project advisor in the work setting.

One page is usually appropriate for an early applicant with limited material; allow a second page when real publications, teaching, and research require it.

Statements, recommendation materials, exhaustive publication lists, grants, committees, and faculty CVs are separate artifacts; do not compress them into this resume schema.
