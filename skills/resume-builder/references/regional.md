# Market conventions

Follow the job's market and employer type, not the candidate's passport or current location.

Settle paper size, normal page range, language, spelling, photo expectations, personal-data expectations, and whether the employer requires a local form rather than an ordinary resume or CV.

Use the posting and the user's experience first; when a convention could materially change the document and remains uncertain, verify current local guidance from an official career service, professional body, or the employer, then state the choice made.

When evidence is inconclusive for an English-language multinational or technology role, use A4 outside North America, US Letter in North America, no photo, no date of birth or marital status, and the posting's language.

Deviate for a photo, nationality, visa line, personal data, unusual length, or local form only when the specific market or employer expects it and the user accepts the tradeoff; the supplied templates do not render photos or jurisdiction-specific forms.

Japanese rirekisho, government-mandated formats, Europass when explicitly required, and exhaustive senior-academic CVs are separate document formats; name that boundary instead of forcing them into this template.

The current routing sanity check recognizes English headings and English month names; a localized document can still be rendered and inspected, but its L1 result must be treated as unsupported rather than translated into confidence.

Keep one target-neutral candidate evidence workspace and create a separate projection for each materially different market or target; facts stay stable while paper, language, ordering emphasis, and accepted personal data may change.
