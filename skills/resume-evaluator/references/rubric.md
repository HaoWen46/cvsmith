# Evaluation rubric

Objective programs establish observable file behavior; this rubric guides the agent's judgment of target fit, practical exposure, and hiring craft.

## Target fit

For each ranked must-have, classify the visible resume evidence:

- `strong`: specific evidence directly addresses the requirement and gives a skeptical interviewer something concrete to probe.
- `credible`: the evidence addresses the requirement naturally but lacks one useful dimension such as scale, outcome, or ownership.
- `weak`: only a skills token or tangential line supports the requirement.
- `absent`: nothing visible supports it.

Treat gates separately from fit: a gate is binary eligibility settled by a date, credential, legal status, clearance, or hard location constraint; years of experience, degree preferences with equivalency language, tools, and seniority are ranked requirements, not gates.

Check whether the strongest page area carries the target's highest-value requirements, whether the posting's vocabulary appears naturally, and whether the evidence is pitched at the decoded level.

## Practical exposure

Use the likely hiring scrutiny, not abstract moral judgment:

- `record-risk blocker`: employer, title, date, degree, credential, publication, award, work authorization, identity, URL, or public artifact likely contradicts a record or reference.
- `interview-risk high`: scope, ownership, metric, or technical wording is likely to collapse under a normal two-minute probe.
- `acceptable framing`: favorable emphasis, compressed wording, a strong verb, or a defensible interpretation that the candidate can explain naturally and that creates no likely external contradiction.

Public verifiability is not required; practical defensibility is.

## CRAFT diagnostic

Use a two-pass read from the rendered page.

Pass 1 is a six-second skim: record the identity, one memorable fact, visual hierarchy, and any immediate noise before rereading.

Pass 2 probes every major claim: what changed, how it changed, what the candidate owned, what evidence anchors the outcome, and what the interviewer will ask next.

Score bands describe the read but never determine readiness:

- `9–10`: the intended identity lands immediately; evidence is specific, differentiated, economical, and probe-ready; no material improvement is visible.
- `7–8`: the identity lands and the page is competitive, but one or two concrete lines, ordering choices, or omissions still leave attainable value on the table.
- `5–6`: the page is plausible but generic, uneven, misallocated, or materially weak against the target.
- `0–4`: the page actively harms the application through confusion, implausibility, severe mismatch, manipulation, or broken presentation.

A score reports quality; the recommendation depends on practical risk and whether a high-value improvement is still accessible.

## Reader and level

Choose the reader implied by the field: a working engineer for SWE, an ML practitioner for AI/ML, faculty or an admissions reader for academic applications, or the field's actual gatekeeper for an unlisted domain.

Read the evidence against the stated level: intern or new-grad needs potential and foundations; junior or mid needs shipped ownership; senior needs ambiguous and cross-team scope; staff or principal needs organizational leverage; manager needs outcomes through people; academic levels follow their own publication, research-direction, and training expectations.

State whether the evidence is below, at, or above the target bar; aiming below demonstrated level may be strategic, so report it as an optional choice unless it creates a real fit problem.

## Page economy

Use measured fill as a diagnostic and the rendered page as the decision; conspicuous emptiness, dense walls, tiny type, weak hierarchy, and orphan spill pages are problems only when they make the document look unfinished or harder to scan.

- Underfull: the page looks unfinished and stronger available evidence could use the space; recover that evidence or redesign the hierarchy.
- Intentionally sparse: the evidence is exhausted, the page still looks finished, and added content would be filler; accept the whitespace.

Do not fix empty space with filler, type inflation, or margins chosen to game a number; recover stronger evidence, improve structure, or accept intentional whitespace when the material is exhausted.

## Finding priority

- `blocker`: requires `DO NOT APPLY` or must be resolved before the application can be viable.
- `high`: a concrete available change likely to improve interview odds or reduce practical exposure; blocks `READY TO SEND` until fixed or shown unavailable.
- `optional`: a real tradeoff or polish item whose expected value is small; never blocks readiness.

A high-value improvement is specific, supported by available material or one focused question, and likely to change target coverage, memorability, credibility, or readability; speculative rewrites and taste-only changes are optional.
